DECISION AFTER REMAND 
A Commission on Professional Competence (Commission) heard this matter by 
videoconference on October 4, 5, 6, 8, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 25, and 28, 2021. The 
Commission’s members were Katherine Everett, Santa Clara County Office of 
Education; Scott Guagliardo, Downtown College Prep (San Jose); and Administrative 
Law Judge Juliet E. Cox, State of California, Office of Administrative Hearings, who 
presided.
Attorneys Brian D. Bock and Amanda S. Georgino represented the Loma Prieta 
Joint Union School District. Attorneys William J. Becker, Jr., and Jeremiah D. Graham 
represented respondent David Kissner, who was present throughout the hearing.
The Commission received oral opening statements, testimonial and 
documentary evidence (including audio and video recordings), and oral closing 
arguments. The record was held open to permit the Commission to review evidence 2 and to deliberate in executive session. The record closed and the matter was 
submitted for decision on November 10, 2021, at the conclusion of the Commission’s 
deliberations. The Commission issued its decision on December 7, 2021.
The Santa Clara County Superior Court issued a Writ of Administrative 
Mandamus on October 11, 2024, to the Commission. This Writ directs the Commission 
to set aside its decision and to reconsider this matter in light of a Statement of 
Decision and Judgment entered by the Santa Clara County Superior Court on 
November 22, 2023. The Writ also directs a return from the Commission to the Santa 
Clara County Superior Court within 150 days after its service on the Commission. 
The parties provided further written argument to the Commission. In addition, 
the complete evidentiary record from the October 2021 hearing, including transcripts, 
was available to the Commission for review, as were the Writ and the Statement of 
Decision and Judgment. Finally, the Commission heard oral argument on remand in 
this matter by videoconference on February 11, 2025. 
The District requested that the Commission take notice of a decision from the 
United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit. Respondent opposed this request 
and asked the Commission to sanction the District. Both the request for official notice 
and the request for sanctions are denied. 
On remand, attorneys Brian D. Bock and Mary Ellyn Gormley represented the 
District. Attorney William J. Becker, Jr., represented respondent David Kissner, who was 
present for the argument. 
This matter was resubmitted for decision on remand on February 11, 2025.

9. The Commission confirms its prior Legal Conclusion 9.
10. The Commission must evaluate “unfitness,” in this context, with reference 
to several criteria, including likelihood and degree of adverse impact on students and 
fellow teachers, proximity or remoteness in time of the relevant conduct, extenuating 
or aggravating circumstances, praiseworthiness or blameworthiness of the teacher’s 
motives, and likelihood of recurrence. ( (1969) 1 
Cal.3d 214, 229-230.) In addition, the Commission must evaluate whether the conduct 
demonstrating unfitness “is caused by a defect in temperament.” (
 (1992) 2 Cal.App.4th 
1429, 1445.) In light of the matters stated below in Legal Conclusions 11 through 19, 
the conduct described in Factual Findings 51 through 69 (refusing to follow Martin’s 
and Fraser’s directives, and teaching a course that was not in the District’s curriculum) 
and 71 through 75 (resisting both supervision and cooperation) establishes 
respondent’s evident unfitness for service. 
Adverse Impact on Students and Fellow Teachers 
11. The matters stated in Factual Findings 52 through 60 threatened 
significant adverse impact to students, who risked missing credit for a key course. The 
matters stated in Factual Findings 61 through 69 confirm that respondent brushed off 
warnings from Fraser regarding potential adverse effects on students from his ”remote 
wilderness school camps.” Finally, the matters stated in Factual Findings 11, 12, 21, 22, 
24, 25, and 74 show that respondent became notorious in the District’s small 
community because of his behavior, undermining his ability to cooperate with fellow 
teachers and with District administrators to serve students.5 12. Although the evidence did not show actual, serious harm to students 
from respondent’s behavior, the evidence overall showed that respondent created 
serious risks of such harm. Moreover, the rancor evident before the Commission did 
reflect damage to the District’s educational community, and the matters stated in 
Factual Findings 11, 12, 18, 21, 22, 24, 25, and 74 show that respondent’s conflict-
seeking behavior was a significant cause of that damage.
Proximity or Remoteness in Time 
13. The matters stated in Factual Findings 51 through 69, 71 through 75, and 
98 through 101 confirm that conduct showing respondent’s unfitness continued until 
and even after his suspension from duty.

Likelihood of Recurrence

Temperament 
19. As summarized in Factual Findings 69, 72, 100, and 101, respondent 
expressed minimal remorse before the Commission for any of the conduct showing his 
unfitness for service. To the contrary, and as summarized in Factual Findings 14, 56, 74, 
98, and 99, and in Legal Conclusions 20 through 23, respondent presented himself to 6 the Commission as someone who deserves respect for his moral courage but who 
instead faces persecution. These matters, all together, make respondent’s unfitness 
evident. 
Retaliation for Constitutionally Protected Activities
20–24. The Commission affirms its prior Legal Conclusions 20 through 24. 
25. The totality of evidence in this matter does not establish an effort by the 
Board or by District administrators to discipline or to dismiss respondent because of 
his opinions or his political activity. To the contrary, all evidence in this matter shows 
that after significant conflict within the District in March and April 2018, the District in 
October 2018 offered respondent the opportunity to save face and move on. 
Respondent emphatically rejected this opportunity. Instead, between October 2018 
and the hearing, respondent made repeated efforts to reopen and revisit the 2018 
conflicts. He also responded to new potential and minor conflicts by amplifying them 
into major conflicts; and he became increasingly insubordinate and uncommunicative 
regarding school activities, even during the novelty and uncertainty of the COVID-19 
pandemic.
26. The Commission considers the matters stated in Factual Findings 51 
through 69 and 71 through 75, and in Legal Conclusions 9 through 19, to justify 
respondent’s dismissal from employment, regardless of his opinions or his 
participation in political activity within the District.
27. The Commission confirms its prior Legal Conclusion 27.
 7 ORDER
Respondent David Kissner is dismissed from his position as a permanent 
certificated employee of the Loma Prieta Joint Union School District due to evident 
unfitness for service.
