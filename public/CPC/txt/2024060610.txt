DECISION 
This matter was heard by the Commission on Professional Competence, 
(Commission) on March 27, 2025 in Los Angeles, California. The Commission consisted 
of Rosario Dougherty, Joshua Friedrich and Glynda B. Gomez, Administrative Law 
Judge (ALJ) with the Office of Administrative Hearings (OAH), State of California. 
Los Angeles Unified School District (District) was represented by Leslie J. Chi, 
Assistant General Counsel and Brian Davenport, Assistant General Counsel. 
Respondent Newton Okewoye (Respondent) was present and represented by 
Shirley A. Lee, Attorney at Law. 2 Both parties presented opening argument, but no evidence was introduced by 
either side. The matter was submitted for decision on March 27, 2025. The Commission 
considered the matter in executive session. After due consideration of the entire 
record herein including official notice of the pleadings and notices on file with OAH, 
the Commission makes the following factual findings, legal conclusions, and order.
FACTUAL FINDINGS 
Parties and Jurisdiction 
1. Respondent was, at all times relevant herein, a permanent certificated 
employee of the District. 
2. On April 16, 2024, District filed a Statement of Charges against 
Respondent, charging him with egregious misconduct (Education Code sections 
44932, subdivision (a)(1) and 44939.10); unprofessional conduct (Education Code 
section 44932, subdivision (a)(2); immoral conduct (Education Code sections 44939); 
and persistent violation of or refusal to obey the school laws of the state or reasonable 
regulations prescribed for the government of the public schools by the State Board of 
Education or by the governing board of the school district employing him (Education 
Code section 44932, subdivision (a)(8).)
3. On June 4, 2024, Respondent filed and served a Request for Hearing and 
Notice of Defense.
4. On July 29, 2024, District filed an Accusation against Respondent, 
charging him with egregious misconduct (Education Code sections 44932, subdivision 
(a)(1) and 44939.100); unprofessional conduct (Education Code section 44932, 3 subdivision (a)(2)); immoral conduct (Education Code sections 44939); and persistent 
violation of or refusal to obey the school laws of the state or reasonable regulations 
prescribed for the government of the public schools by the State Board of Education 
or by the governing board of the school district employing him (Education Code 
section 44932, subdivision (a)(8).)
5. Neither party provided any evidence at the administrative hearing.
LEGAL CONCLUSIONS 
Jurisdiction 
1. The Commission has jurisdiction to proceed in this matter under 
Education Code section 44944.
Burden and Standard of Proof 
2. In this case, the District has the burden of proving its allegations by a 
preponderance of the evidence. (
(1985) 164 Cal.App.3d 1035, 1039-1040.) Preponderance of the evidence means that 
“the evidence on [the District’s] side outweighs, preponderates over, is more than, the 
evidence on the other side.” ( (1990) 226 Cal.App.3d 314, 
325.)
Disposition 
3. District failed to establish any evidence that Respondent committed 
egregious misconduct within the meaning of Education Code sections 44932, 
subdivision (a)(1) and 44939.10. 4 4. District failed to establish any evidence that Respondent committed 
unprofessional conduct within the meaning of Education Code section 44932, 
subdivision (a)(2). 
5. District failed to establish any evidence that Respondent engaged in 
immoral conduct within the meaning of Education Code sections 44939. 
6. District failed to establish any evidence that Respondent engaged in 
persistent violation of or refused to obey the school laws of the state or reasonable 
regulations prescribed for the government of the public schools by the State Board of 
Education or by the governing board of the school district employing him within the 
meaning of Education Code section 44932, subdivision (a)(8).
